# Northwind Atlas Product FAQ

## About This FAQ

This FAQ helps employees answer common customer questions about the Northwind Atlas, our flagship autonomous warehouse robot. Atlas carries totes and pallets between stations without human guidance. If a customer asks for something not listed here, forward the question to the Solutions team.

## Performance

### How long does the battery last?

Atlas runs for about 10 hours on a single charge under typical warehouse conditions. A full recharge takes 90 minutes at the standard dock. Heavy payloads and constant climbing of ramps can shorten the runtime by up to 20 percent.

### How much can it carry?

The maximum payload is 25 kilograms on the standard tray. A heavy-duty tray accessory is in development, and customers should not exceed the stated payload because overload sensors stop the robot automatically.

### How fast does it move?

The top speed is 2.5 meters per second in open aisles. In areas where people are detected, Atlas slows to 0.8 meters per second and keeps a one meter safety margin. The robot can climb ramps with a slope of up to 8 degrees.

### What temperatures can it handle?

Atlas operates between minus 10 and 45 degrees Celsius, and storage in cold rooms is supported down to minus 20 degrees. The robot has an IP54 rating, which protects it from dust and splashing water but not from full submersion.

## Software

### Which software version is current?

The current software release is AtlasOS 4.2. It added improved route planning, support for two-robot handovers, and a new dashboard for fleet managers. Customers running AtlasOS 3.x can upgrade directly without a service visit.

### How often are firmware updates released?

Firmware updates ship every 6 weeks on a fixed Tuesday. Each release is tested for two weeks in our own warehouse before it reaches customers. Critical security fixes may be delivered outside this schedule and are announced by email to customer administrators.

Customers may postpone any non-security update by up to two release cycles.

## Sensors and Navigation

Atlas uses a lidar sensor with a range of 30 meters, plus four depth cameras for detecting obstacles at floor level. It builds a map of the facility during a setup run that takes about one day for a typical 5,000 square meter warehouse.

The robot can operate without a network connection for up to 4 hours using its stored map, although fleet coordination is paused during that time.

## Warranty and Support

### What does the warranty cover?

The standard warranty covers 24 months from delivery and includes parts and labor for defects in materials and workmanship. Batteries are covered for 12 months or 800 charge cycles, whichever comes first. Damage from misuse, such as driving the robot off a loading dock, is excluded.

Extended warranty plans of up to 5 years are sold by the Sales Operations team.

### When can customers reach support?

Customer support is available from 7 AM to 7 PM Central Time, Monday through Friday. Customers with a premium plan receive 24 hour coverage and a named support engineer. Support tickets are opened through the customer portal or by phone, and the first response target is 2 hours for urgent cases.

## Pricing and Ordering

The list price of a single Atlas robot is 48,000 dollars, with volume discounts beginning at orders of 10 units. Standard lead time is 8 weeks from purchase order to delivery. Employees should never quote discounts directly, and should pass such requests to the Sales Operations team.

## Safety

Atlas has two emergency stop buttons and a wireless stop that works from 50 meters away. The robot meets the major international warehouse safety standards, and our compliance documents are available on the customer portal.
